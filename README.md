# PayRevive — measured revenue recovery for failed payments

**Razorpay AI Buildathon 2026 — Track 03 (AI Revenue Recovery)**

The brief asks for one thing above all else:

> Don't just identify the problem. Show measured money recovered across a batch, with
> compliant escalation, stopping rules, and an audit trail.

So here is the measurement first, and the architecture afterwards.

## Measured money recovered

39,200 failed payments worth **₹75.06 cr**, over 500 batch runs — 5 policies × 5
scenarios × 20 seeds. Every policy saw the byte-identical batch at every seed: same
customers, same bank outages, same coin flips. Each row is therefore a *paired
difference* against doing nothing, not two separate experiments compared after the fact.

| Policy | Lift per batch, 95% CI | Share of achievable | Compliance refusals | Instruments broken |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `do_nothing` | ₹0 | 0.0% | 0 | 0 |
| `naive_retry` — retry until it works | ₹6.04 L [₹5.34, ₹6.74] | 23.6% | 8,800 of 46,174 | 0.80% |
| `rules` — a good hand-written incumbent | ₹11.70 L [₹10.80, ₹12.63] | 45.6% | 2,445 of 56,053 | 0.28% |
| **`payrevive` — this submission** | **₹17.52 L [₹16.45, ₹18.61]** | **68.3%** | **0** | **0.21%** |
| `oracle` — reads hidden state, not a proposal | ₹25.64 L [₹24.39, ₹26.93] | 100.0% | 753 of 26,738 | 0.03% |

`payrevive` recovers **50% more money than the incumbent while breaking fewer working
cards**, and it is the only policy on the ladder that takes zero actions the deployed
compliance engine would refuse. Full tables, per-scenario and per-root-cause, are in
[`backend/reports/REPORT.md`](backend/reports/REPORT.md), regenerated by one command.

## Why the number is worth believing

**It is a counterfactual, not a recovery rate.** A recovery rate counts customers who
would have paid anyway. Lift is `policy − do_nothing` on the same seed, so the policy is
credited only with money that would not otherwise have arrived.

**The outcome is never a function of the label.** In the simulator a charge succeeds or
fails according to physical preconditions — is the instrument alive, does the account
hold the amount, is the bank up at that minute. `_charge_succeeds` never reads
`true_cause`. A policy cannot win by guessing the label, only by acting well.

**Common random numbers.** Each event type draws from its own RNG stream, and both
branches draw whether or not the draw is used, so a policy taking one extra action does
not shift the luck of every payment after it.

**Bootstrap intervals, not t-based.** Rupee lift sums a heavy-tailed amount
distribution where one payment can be several percent of a batch, so intervals are
resampled 10,000× from the paired per-seed differences. The seed is fixed; regenerating
the report reproduces the bounds exactly.

**The headline is measured twice by routes that share no arithmetic** — once as the
paired difference, once from the environment's private verdict on causation. Every run
satisfies `lift ≤ attributed recovery`; the gap is money collected on Monday that would
have arrived on Wednesday, which is real, valuable, and deliberately excluded from the
headline.

**Every action is replayed through the production compliance engine.** Not a
re-implementation — `app/execution/compliance.py`, the same pure function the live API
calls. Lift earned by an action production would refuse is lift that cannot be banked.
Publishing that column is what dropped the headline from 71.5% to 68.3%, and the drop is
the point.

## What it refuses to claim

A payment recovery system can inflate its own numbers three ways without writing a single
line of dishonest code. All three are closed here.

**Attribution.** A customer who was always going to pay on Wednesday pays on Wednesday;
we happened to message them on Monday. That is `AMBIGUOUS`, and it is never a win.
`SYSTEM_RECOVERED` requires the customer to have paid *on our link* — Razorpay names the
link in the `payment_link.paid` webhook, so causation is recorded rather than inferred.
The 6-hour ambiguity window is one constant, asserted equal in production and simulator
by a test, because a wider window in production would book rupees the report excluded.

**The bandit's reward.** An ambiguous recovery teaches the bandit *nothing* —
`attribution.reward()` returns `None`, not `0.0`. Scoring it a win would teach the policy
that messaging people who were about to pay anyway is the best available strategy; scoring
it a loss would tell a Beta posterior the arm failed when the experiment simply produced
no readable result.

**Harm.** Retrying a card that is merely out of funds can get it blocked by the issuer.
The report counts working instruments killed by our own retries as a share of those alive
to be broken, so "recovered more money" cannot be bought with customers left worse off
than if nothing had been done.

## Which of the brief's directions this took, and which it did not

The brief lists seven example directions. They are alternatives, not a checklist, and the
bar underneath them asks for one loop measured end to end rather than seven demonstrated
shallowly. Each additional direction would need its own counterfactual, its own compliance
gates, its own stopping rules and its own audit events before it could count as *measured*
money — so this submission took the first direction and went the whole way down it.

| Direction | Here |
| --- | --- |
| Payment degradation → root cause → recovery action | **The spine.** Classifier over 7 causes with per-payment SHAP evidence, a policy that prices actions under a compliance veto, execution, audit trail, webhook attribution, and the measured lift above. |
| Mandate retry sequencer | **Inside the decision.** `is_recurring` → `has_mandate` exempts autopay from the ₹10,000 auto-retry ceiling; 3 retries maximum, 15 minutes apart; and `payrevive` prices the 3.5% chance that each failed retry pauses the mandate, which is why it retries less than the incumbent and breaks fewer instruments. |
| Failed-subscription recovery | **Half.** Mandate state and `mandate_expired` failures are modelled; there is no subscription object, billing cycle or dunning schedule. |
| Checkout drop-off recovery | No. Every path here starts from a *failed payment*; abandoned checkouts emit no failure to diagnose. |
| B2B receivables chaser | No. No invoices, no ageing, no promise objects. |
| Hinglish voice recovery | No. Channel and tone are decision variables the bandit learns over; there is no voice channel and no language generation. |
| Promise-to-pay tracker | No. `SCHEDULED_RETRY` and the 72-hour window are the nearest thing, and neither records a customer commitment. |

## The decision path

Each stage exists because the one before it cannot answer the question.

**1 · Root cause — XGBoost + SHAP.** Razorpay's error fields say *what the gateway
returned*, not *why the payment failed*. `BAD_REQUEST_ERROR` covers a dead card, a full
account and a bank outage alike. The classifier infers the latent cause across 17 features
(bank health this hour, customer history, time of day, amount bucket), and SHAP attaches
the reasoning to every prediction — so an escalation to a human arrives with the evidence
rather than a score.

**2 · Strategy — Thompson-sampling contextual bandit.** Given a cause, which action?
Context is `root cause × method × amount bucket`; the posterior is a Beta per arm, updated
only from outcomes that survived attribution. It adapts to a bank whose success rate
changed this morning, which no static table does.

**3 · Value pricing — `payrevive`.** The bandit ranks actions; something has to decide
whether the best one is worth taking *at all*. Every candidate is priced as
`P(recovery) × amount − cost`, against a recoverability model **fitted from action logs**
rather than hand-tuned. Escalation is priced specially: it is terminal, so its true cost
is not the ₹35 call, it is every cheaper action the payment will now never get.

**4 · LLM — Gemini Flash, on the cases that earn it.** Invoked for high-value or
low-confidence decisions, where the marginal reasoning is worth the latency and the spend.
It cannot override compliance; it proposes, and the engine still decides.

**5 · Compliance — a pure function, and a hard veto.** Quiet hours (22:00–08:00 IST),
daily contact caps, retry limits and intervals, per-rail amount ceilings (UPI's ₹1 lakh
NPCI limit; cards and netbanking deliberately absent, because per-issuer limits are not
observable). `evaluate()` takes no clock, no Redis and no database — the decision instant
is an argument — so any verdict can be re-derived from its own audit row months later.

**6 · Every block leads somewhere.** A block is almost never "never" — it is "not now" or
"not that way", and the engine names which. A payment blocked at 23:50 by quiet hours is
requeued to wake at 08:05 IST, computed from the wall clock rather than a fixed backoff,
and its session stays `OPEN`: a sleeping payment has not failed. A ₹1.75 L failure blocked
by UPI's ceiling is offered a rail that can carry it. Substituted actions are re-checked
before they run, so satisfying one rule cannot breach another.

**7 · Stopping rules.** `GIVE_UP` is a first-class action and can never be blocked — a
stopping rule that compliance could refuse would be a policy with no way to stop. A payment
with nothing legal left is written to the exception list a human works, not dissolved into
a recovery rate where an abandoned payment reads identically to an unrecoverable one.

**8 · Audit trail.** Append-only; no `UPDATE`, no `DELETE`. Ingestion, classification with
its SHAP vector, strategy and who chose it, the compliance verdict and what it blocked, the
remedy taken and when it will wake, the execution, the observed outcome and its attribution
verdict *with the sentence that justifies it*. An attribution is a claim about causation
made by software; the reviewer needs to see the number of hours it turned on.

## The diagnosis, measured against what it has to beat

An accuracy figure on its own is not a claim about anything. A classifier fed error fields
that determine their own cause reports 95% and has diagnosed nothing — it inverted a lookup
table the author wrote. So `app/sim/emission.py` overlaps the emission distributions on
purpose (`payment_failed` appears under all seven causes; BANK_DOWNTIME and
NETWORK_TRANSIENT are near-identical on the wire), and the accuracy is reported next to the
ceiling those fields impose:

| | Accuracy |
| --- | --- |
| Predict the most common cause every time (`INSUFFICIENT_FUNDS`) | 22.70% |
| **Bayes-optimal from the error fields alone** — computed analytically over 179 signatures | **68.38%** |
| XGBoost given only `error_source`, `error_step`, `error_reason` | 69.22% ± 0.60% |
| **All 17 features** | **82.80%** (macro F1 0.823) |

The third row is the one that matters: an ablation restricted to the error fields cannot
beat their Bayes bound, so landing *at* it — 1.4 standard errors above, on a finite test
set — is what says the fit is sound rather than leaking. The **14.42 points** the full model
adds come from bank health, customer history and timing, because there is no other input.

16,000 training payments and 6,000 held out, split **by seed** rather than by row: payments
inside one generated batch share the outage windows and the customers of the rows next to
them, so an intra-batch cut scores the model on circumstances it was fitted on. Labels are
the simulator's latent `true_cause` — never `recovery_sessions.root_cause`, which the worker
overwrites with the model's own prediction. Features are built by the production
`FeatureExtractor`, not a copy, so there is no train/serve skew to go unnoticed.

Trained on a normal week, scored on weeks it never saw: `outage_day` 78.15%, `salary_week`
81.65%, `festival_spike` 79.99%, `stress_dead_instruments` 81.40%. The weakest class is
`NETWORK_TRANSIENT` (F1 0.692), confused mostly with `BANK_DOWNTIME` — exactly the pair
`emission.py` made separable only by correlated failures, which is the feature having to do
real work.

## What is real and what is simulated

Honesty here matters more than the appearance of completeness.

| Real | Simulated |
| --- | --- |
| Razorpay webhook ingestion, HMAC-verified, idempotent via Redis `SET NX` | Card auto-retry — no tokenized cards in a test account, so the charge is mocked |
| Payment link creation through the Razorpay API | Bank downtime feed — the poller's shape is real, the data is generated |
| Supabase persistence, Redis feature store and bandit posteriors | Failed-payment traffic, generated with Indian distributions (UPI share, salary windows, correlated outages) |
| Compliance engine — the same function the eval replays | |
| Attribution and the bandit update, driven by `payment.captured` / `payment_link.paid` | |

The eval harness runs **fully in-process** with no cloud dependency. The product runs on
hosted Supabase and Redis Cloud; the evaluation has to be reproducible by anyone who
clones the repo, so it shares no infrastructure with the running system.

## Reproducing the measurement

No credentials, no cloud, no network. This is the claim, and it is one command:

```bash
cd backend && pip install -r requirements.txt && python -m app.eval
```

That regenerates `backend/reports/REPORT.md` from scratch — 500 batch runs, a few minutes —
and every number in the table above reappears identically. Verified: re-running it produces
a file that differs from the committed one in the generation timestamp and nothing else,
because the scenario seeds and the bootstrap seed are both fixed.

The test suite:

```bash
cd backend && python -m pytest tests/ -q
```

367 tests, ~3 minutes. They are mostly not unit tests. They assert the properties the
measurement depends on: that the simulator's outcome never reads the label, that lift never
exceeds attributed recovery, that the ambiguity window is identical on both sides, that
`payrevive` takes no action the compliance engine would refuse, that every remedy the
engine can recommend has somewhere in the worker to go, that a paged read of a table larger
than PostgREST's row cap comes back whole and in a fixed order, and that the classifier's
accuracy is measured against the bound it has to beat rather than reported on its own.

The classifier is a separate fit, and it is not optional:

```bash
cd backend && python -m app.ml.train
```

A fresh clone has `metrics.json` but no model, and `worker.process_failed_payment` returns
early without one — no payment gets classified, no strategy gets chosen, and nothing errors.
Roughly a minute. It writes the model, both encoders and the metrics into
`app/ml/model_artifacts/`, and prints the four numbers below.

## Running the live system

```bash
cd backend
pip install -r requirements.txt
cp .env.example .env      # Supabase, Redis Cloud, Razorpay test, Gemini keys
uvicorn app.main:app --reload
```

```bash
arq app.worker.WorkerSettings
```

```bash
cd frontend && npm install && npm run dev
```

Then: `POST /api/v1/batch/generate` to create synthetic failures,
`POST /api/v1/model/train` to fit the classifier if `python -m app.ml.train` has not been run
(same code path, and the worker silently classifies nothing without a model),
`POST /api/v1/batch/run?limit=15` to put open sessions through the real pipeline. Sessions
stay `OPEN` until Razorpay reports a capture or a paid link — outcomes are observed, never
assumed.

`limit` bounds how many sessions one call works, and `0` means all of them. It exists
because honest work is slow work: every payment costs a classifier call, a compliance read,
a bandit read, an action and several audit inserts, each a round trip to a hosted database.
A measured run over 137 open sessions took **434.7 seconds**; fifteen takes about 22. The
response carries `open_at_start` and `not_worked_this_run` and the dashboard prints the
second one, because `processed: 15` on a queue of 97 reads exactly like a finished batch.

Two endpoints exist for reading the run rather than triggering it.
`GET /api/v1/compliance/policy` serves the limits the engine enforces, each with the reason
it exists, so the stopping rules can be read without reading the source.
`GET /api/v1/dashboard/exceptions` is the queue of payments the system stopped working and
why — a compliance block with no reachable remedy, an execution failure, or an exhausted
gateway allowance. `POST /api/v1/sandbox/outcomes` delivers the callbacks Razorpay's test
mode never sends, and refuses to run when `APP_ENV=production`, because a tool that
fabricates outcomes must not be reachable where outcomes are real.

### What a test key runs out of

Razorpay's test mode allows **30 payment links per account for the lifetime of the
account**, and refuses the 31st with `429` — the same status it uses for "you are going too
fast". Two consequences, both handled:

- The retry loop used to treat it as a throttle and spend four attempts and eight seconds of
  backoff per payment on an answer that could never change. `is_quota_exhausted` tells the
  two apart on the response description, narrowly enough that a genuine throttle is still
  retried, and the executor files an exhausted allowance as `GATEWAY_QUOTA_EXHAUSTED`
  rather than `EXECUTION_ERROR` — a credential that has run out, not a bug in the code.
- `PAYMENT_LINK_SENT` is the only audit event that can produce a `SYSTEM_RECOVERED` verdict,
  so once the allowance is gone the live `provably_ours` figure stops moving. On this key it
  is 5 sessions. That is a fact about the credential, which is exactly why the headline
  number comes from the eval harness, where a counterfactual exists.

Writes are also spaced 0.4 s apart under a lock. Before that, a batch of forty link
creations arrived inside four seconds and Razorpay answered `429` to nearly all of them —
silently, since the client returned `None` and the run still reported success.

## Known limitations

Stated plainly, because a reviewer will find them anyway and the ones below are the honest
edges of the work rather than the things it hides.

- **The classifier's hardest class is genuinely hard, and stays that way.**
  `NETWORK_TRANSIENT` and `BANK_DOWNTIME` emit almost the same error fields; separating them
  rests on one noisy feature, the concurrent-failure spike. F1 0.692 on the former is the
  honest cost of not letting the simulator's own downtime variable into the feature vector.
- **The bandit's features are re-extracted at reward time, not stored at decision time.**
  Bank health has moved in between, so a contextual arm is updated against slightly
  different context than it chose under. The fix is storing the feature vector on the
  session — a schema change.
- **`oracle` is a ceiling, not a competitor.** It reads hidden state and is listed to bound
  what is achievable. It also sends quiet-hour messages, which nothing forbids; that is
  reported rather than hidden.
- **Per-issuer card ceilings are unobservable**, so no ceiling is enforced on cards or
  netbanking. UPI's ₹1 lakh is enforced because NPCI publishes it.
- **The live dashboard cannot show a lift, and does not pretend to.** It separates recoveries
  into paid-on-our-link, came-back-on-their-own and cause-not-established, and reports the
  share only over the sessions whose cause an audit event establishes — 217 legacy rows are
  excluded from the numerator rather than assumed to be ours. The measured figure lives on
  `/analytics`, from the harness, because a lift needs a twin batch that production does not
  run.
- **A total read from one page is a wrong number wearing the right label.** PostgREST caps an
  unbounded `select()` at 1,000 rows and returns the truncated set with no error; a census of
  this project's 1,158 `audit_events` summed to exactly 1,000 before it was fixed. Reads page
  now, and every page names a sort column, because `LIMIT/OFFSET` over an unordered scan can
  return the same row twice or skip it.
